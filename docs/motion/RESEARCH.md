# Motion research: making Tavi feel alive on stream

Concise version, written to drive `src/renderer/motion/`. Numbers marked _heuristic_ are engineering choices, not sourced facts. Implementation parameters live in `profile.ts`, `systems/*.ts`.

## Why the previous renderer read as static

It scrubbed authored clips to fixed frames (e.g. `thoughtful` frozen at t=0.6), used one smoothstep envelope per action, a ±0.3% sine "breath", and an exponential glide for movement. A frozen pose "instantly appears dead"; animators keep holds alive with breath, eye darts and drift ("moving holds", [Animation Mentor](https://animationmentor.com/blog/why-all-animators-need-to-master-the-moving-hold)). The asset audit (ASSET-AUDIT.md) also found that the face/brow/mouth/blink "tracks" looked inactive because they are joystick channels the runtime re-applies every frame, not because the rig lacks them.

## Key findings

1. **Never freeze; layer slow, incommensurate life.** Live2D's default breath combines periods 3.2/3.5/5.5/6.5/15.5 s so the loop never repeats ([Cubism Breath](https://docs.live2d.com/en/cubism-sdk-manual/breath/)). Resting adults breathe 12-20/min ([ALA](https://www.lung.org/blog/respiratory-rate-vital-signs)). Duolingo's Lily combines 8 head x 8 body idles to avoid visible loops ([Rive blog](https://rive.app/blog/duolingo-s-ai-powered-video-call-brings-lily-to-life)).
2. **Blinks:** ~17/min at rest, 26/min in conversation, 4.5/min reading ([Bentivoglio 1997](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.870120629)); close ~2x faster than open ([Evinger 1991](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/eyelid-movements-mechanisms-and-normal-data/)); asymmetric, fully closing blinks read more natural, also on cartoon faces ([Trutoiu 2011](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/modeling-and-animating-eye-blinks/)); they cluster on large gaze shifts ([Evinger 1994](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/not-looking-while-leaping-the-linkage-of-blinking-and-saccadic-ga/)). Live2D defaults: close 0.1 s, closed 0.05 s, open 0.15 s, interval uniform 0-7 s ([CubismEyeBlink](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Live2D/CubismWebFramework/develop/src/effect/cubismeyeblink.ts)).
3. **Gaze:** saccade duration 25 ms + 2.4 ms/deg, 90% under 15 deg; listening gaze holds are long, gaze-away glances 0.4-0.9 s ([Eyes Alive, Lee et al. 2002](https://repository.upenn.edu/hms/51)); eyes lead, the head joins beyond ~15-20 deg ([Ruhland et al. 2015](https://graphics.cs.wisc.edu/Papers/2015/RPABBGMM15/Ruhland_et_al-2015-Computer_Graphics_Forum.pdf)).
4. **Second-order dynamics** give anticipation/overshoot/follow-through from three numbers: f (Hz), zeta, r (r<0 anticipates) ([t3ssel8r 2022](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KPoeNZZ6H4s); [port](https://gist.github.com/ttalexander2/3b8743bf2d38d40a017e807aab43c016)). Critically damped springs by half-life are exact for any dt ([Holden, Spring-It-On](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)). Use inertialization / dead blending (~0.2 s) instead of crossfades for cuts ([Bollo GDC 2018](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025165/Inertialization); [Holden, Dead Blending](https://theorangeduck.com/page/dead-blending)).
5. **12 principles for a tiny character:** stage one idea at a time, change the expression before or after a move, never during; eyes lead the head; parts drag in proportion to weight; avoid mirrored "twins" ([Lasseter 1987](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~fowlkes/class/ics162/exercises/lasseter.pdf)). A hold of ~8 frames (24 fps) is a beat, ~24 is deliberate ([DigiCel](https://digicel.net/animation-holds/)). Comedic beat order for "skeptical eyebrow, then exaggerated nod": expression change, hold, then the move with anticipation.
6. **At ~220 px tall** silhouette and whole-head/body motion carry; brows are a few pixels (_heuristic_, consistent with Lasseter's silhouette staging and [Valve TF2](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2008/GDC2008_StylizationWithAPurpose_TF2.pdf)). Keep idle low amplitude so Tavi does not upstage the stream (Clippy lesson).
7. **Personality:** Laban/EMOTE effort maps to timing (sudden vs sustained), overshoot (free flow), anticipation (strong weight) ([Chi et al. 2000](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~badler/siggraph00/emote.pdf)). Extraversion = larger (x1.2-1.4), faster (80% duration), more frequent motion and posture shifts; introversion = 0.5-0.6 scale, slower, longer latency, gaze aversion ([Neff et al. 2010](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~neff/papers/extraversion_final_PRE_PRINT.pdf)). Sarcastic/deadpan characters need dialled-back expressions ([Duolingo Lily](https://rive.app/blog/duolingo-s-ai-powered-video-call-brings-lily-to-life)).
8. **Locomotion:** drive the gait phase by distance (stride wheel; [Rosen GDC 2014](https://www.gameanim.com/?p=15568), [technique](https://discussions.unity.com/threads/surveyor-wheel-technique.251435/)) so feet never slide. Cadence: brisk 2 Hz, stroll 1.5 Hz, tired 1.0-1.2 Hz ([Monmouth walk timing](https://animation.monmouth.edu/instruct/?p=351)); body lowest just after contact, highest at passing. Lean = gain x atan2(a, g), gain 0.3-0.7 ([Vulkan tutorial](https://docs.vulkan.org/tutorial/latest/Advanced_glTF/Procedural_Animation_IK/06_physics_driven_lean.html)). Reynolds "arrive" with a slowing radius ([Reynolds 1999](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/steer/gdc99/)). Sad/tired gait: slower, head inclined, smaller amplitude; happy: faster, bigger ([Roether 2009](https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2204009); [Montepare 1987](https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/BF00999605)). Flip facing instantly and mask it with a brief squash ([Celeste Player.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NoelFB/Celeste/master/Source/Player/Player.cs)).
9. **Squash and stretch** preserve volume (scaleX ~ 1/scaleY). Celeste uses takeoff (0.6, 1.4) and landing up to (1.6, 0.4) on a 10 px sprite with ~0.23 s recovery (Player.cs); for a 220 px chibi use 6-15% stretch, 8-20% landing squash, up to ~30% for a drop-in (_heuristic_).
10. **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.2.2 requires pause/stop/hide for auto-moving content over 5 s ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)); vestibular triggers are scaling, spinning, parallax and large horizontal motion ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/7551/responsive-design-for-motion/)). Reduced motion keeps blinks, slow small breath, small saccades and crossfades; drops travel, bounces, big squash and shake. Overlay viewers' OS settings do not reach OBS, so the streamer's quiet/reduced toggles are the control. Never shake the whole stream canvas.

## Parameters used (see `profile.ts`)

|                     | cozy         | playful           | shy         |
| ------------------- | ------------ | ----------------- | ----------- |
| tempo / amplitude   | 0.85 / 0.85  | 1.2 / 1.2         | 0.9 / 0.65  |
| head spring f, zeta | 1.4 Hz, 0.85 | 2.2 Hz, 0.45      | 1.6 Hz, 0.8 |
| breath period       | 4.6 s        | 3.2 s             | 3.9 s       |
| blink mean          | 4.2 s        | 3.2 s             | 2.8 s       |
| reaction latency    | 0.25 s       | 0.12 s            | 0.4 s       |
| gaze-away bias      | 0.3          | 0.35              | 0.65        |
| fidget mean         | 14 s         | 8 s               | 11 s        |
| gait                | amble        | hop (long) / step | scoot       |
| entrance weights    | tired walk   | drop              | climb       |

Attitude: gentle (r=0, softer overshoot), cheeky (r=-0.35 anticipation), spicy (r=-0.5, f x1.25, overshoot x1.35). Energy 0-100: tempo 0.75-1.25x, amplitude 0.6-1.3x, fidget rate 0.5-1.6x (_heuristic_). Quiet: no fidgets, 0.6x amplitude. Reduced motion: no travel (fade relocate), squash <= 0.1, amplitude <= 0.5, no overshoot.

Blink shape: close 75 ms (accelerating), closed 45 ms, open 170 ms; right eye trails 12 ms. Gait (body heights, BH): step stride 0.12 at 2.8 Hz, amble 0.11 at 2.2 Hz, scoot 0.07 at 2.6 Hz, tired 0.09 at 1.7 Hz, hop 0.3 BH at 2.2 Hz with apex 12% BH. Steps are short because the chibi's foot IK reaches only ~0.08 BH.

## Rive notes

- Runtime docs for agents: [rive.app/docs/llms.txt](https://rive.app/docs/llms.txt); [low-level API](https://rive.app/docs/runtimes/web/low-level-api-usage.md): `anim.advance(dt); anim.apply(mix)`, then `artboard.advance(dt)`.
- Verified in `@rive-app/webgl2` 2.45.0 typings: `artboard.node/bone/rootBone/transformComponent(name)` expose rotation/scale (and x/y on Node/RootBone) for procedural writes; joystick values have no runtime API; `LinearAnimation.duration/fps` are methods on some objects (reading them as fields gives NaN).
- State machine inputs are deprecated in favour of data binding ([migration guide](https://rive.app/docs/editor/data-binding/migration-guide.md)); joysticks scrub timelines from x/y ([docs](https://rive.app/docs/editor/manipulating-shapes/joysticks.md)); additive blend states mix poses additively ([states](https://rive.app/docs/editor/state-machine/states.md)).
- Rive publishes an official agent skill (rive-app/skills, MIT). It is a discovery/marketing note without runtime detail, so it was not vendored. Rive also ships a CLI that builds .riv files from text for coding agents; installing it is a user decision.

## Authoring spec for a proper Tavi rig (for a Rive animator)

Our manifests consume these names; anything missing falls back to procedural motion.

- **Export names** for every component listed here (exported runtime names; the current file strips all names).
- **Bones:** `root` (hips, RootBone), `spine`, `neck`, `head`; `arm_L_upper/lower`, `arm_R_upper/lower`; `leg_L_upper/lower`, `leg_R_upper/lower`; `hair_*` chains (2-3 bones each) for follow-through. IK constraints on legs targeting nodes `foot_L_target`, `foot_R_target`, and on arms targeting `hand_L_target`, `hand_R_target`.
- **View model `Tavi`** (data binding, preferred over SM inputs), numbers in -1..1 unless noted: `gazeX`, `gazeY`, `headTilt`, `browL`, `browR`, `blinkL`, `blinkR` (0..1), `squintL`, `squintR` (0..1), `smile`, `mouthOpen` (0..1), `viseme` (enum, later), `squash`, `lean`, `speed` (0..1 of max walk), `direction` (-1/1), `energy` (0..1). Map each to joysticks/timelines in the file as the current rig already does for face channels.
- **State machine `Motion`, layers (bottom wins):** `Locomotion` (1D blend on `speed`: idle 0, walk 0.5, run/hop 1; `turn` trigger plays `turn_L/R`; `start` and `stop` one-shots), `Body` (additive blend for lean/squash/breath), `Arms` (gesture one-shots `wave`, `point`, `celebrate`, `dust_off`, `stretch`, `yawn`), `Face` (additive blend of the face numbers above), `Blink` (driven by `blinkL/R`; no baked blink loops in body clips).
- **Clips (60 fps, names exact):** `walk_cycle` (one stride pair, contact frame at 0, measured `strideLength` in artboard units noted in the clip description), `run_cycle`, `hop_cycle`, `tired_walk`, `turn_L`, `turn_R`, `start_walk`, `stop_walk`; entrances/exits `fall_loop`, `land`, `dust_off`, `climb_grip_L/R` (hands gripping the bottom edge), `pull_up`, `yawn`, `sigh`, `peek_in`, `duck`. Keep loops short, body clips free of face keys, and keep authored clips additive-friendly (key offsets from the rest pose).
- **Rest pose:** a timeline `rest` that keys every joystick and bone to neutral (the current file's `rig testing` serves this role).
- Manifest entry consumed by the motion layer: `{ gaits: { walk: { clip: 'walk_cycle', strideLength, speed } }, clips: { wave: 'wave', ... }, nodes: { footL: 'foot_L_target', ... } }`. With a real walk cycle, locomotion plays it at rate = speed / strideLength instead of the procedural feet.
